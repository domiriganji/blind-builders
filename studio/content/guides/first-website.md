## The short version

1. Open [Build](/build) and describe the website in your own words.
2. Listen to the read-back. Correct it, or tell the AI to go ahead.
3. Wait on the job page. Use "Check again", or wait for the three taps.
4. Read the result page: what was built, what the site checked, and what nobody has checked yet.
5. Open the preview and move through your site with your screen reader.
6. Ask for one change at a time.
7. Ask for a peer check, so another blind member tests it with their own screen reader.

## Before you start: what to say

The AI builds from what you tell it, so a good description saves time. Say:

- What the website is for, in one sentence. For example: "A website for my cake shop, so people can see what I bake and how to order."
- Who will use it.
- Which pages it needs. For example: home, cakes and prices, how to order, contact.
- The words that must appear exactly: your business name, opening hours, prices, address.
- Anything it must not have. For example: no pictures that only show text, no music that plays by itself.

What can be built here: websites, phone web apps, small tools and games made of web pages. They have no server, so they cannot take payments, send email or messages, or keep accounts with sign-in. Anything they save stays in the visitor's own browser. If your idea needs one of these, the read-back should say so and describe the closest thing that can be built. If it does not, ask.

You do not need to know how websites are made. Plain words are fine.

## Step 1: describe it and listen to the read-back

Open [Build](/build) and type or dictate your description. The AI does not build anything yet. First it reads back what it understood: the purpose, the pages, and what goes on each one.

Listen for three things:

- Is anything missing that you asked for?
- Did it add anything you did not ask for?
- Are names, prices and times exactly right?

If anything is wrong, say so in your own words. When the read-back is right, tell it to go ahead.

## Step 2: wait for the build

Building takes a little time, so you get a job page. Its heading starts with "Working on it". Activate "Check again" to see if it is done, or, if JavaScript is on, wait for three short taps: your focus moves to a link to the result. You can leave the page and come back through [Your recent work](/jobs).

## Step 3: read the result

The result page puts the summary first. It tells you:

- what was built, or what changed since the last version;
- what the site checked automatically, and the result of each check;
- what the site could not check, and still needs a person.

## Step 4: open the preview

The result page has a link to open the preview of your website. Explore it the way a visitor would:

- Jump through the headings. Is there exactly one level 1 heading, and does it say what the page is?
- Tab through every link and button. Does each one say what it does when you hear it on its own?
- Fill in any form. Does every field announce a label? If you leave a required field empty, are you told what is wrong?
- Check the page title your screen reader announces when the page loads.

The preview runs separately from this site, so a mistake in your website cannot read your sign-in to this site.

## Step 5: understand the checks

The site checks the HTML of each page automatically. These checks look for:

- a language set on the page, and a page title;
- exactly one main heading, and headings that do not skip levels;
- text alternatives on images;
- labels on form fields, and names on buttons and links;
- no repeated ids, no blocked zooming, and a main region;
- titles on frames, no forced tab order, no page that refreshes by itself;
- header cells on data tables, no sound that plays by itself;
- a page light enough for slow phones.

These checks read the code. They cannot hear a screen reader or see the screen. They do not check how a real screen reader speaks the page, whether the page looks right to sighted visitors, colour contrast, zooming to 400 percent on a phone, or whether buttons do what their names say. That is why peer checks exist.

Taylor Arndt, a blind developer who tests apps made with AI, keeps finding the same problems: buttons announced only as "button", pages with no headings, and focus that never moves after an action ([Taylor Arndt](https://taylorarndt.substack.com/p/everybody-is-vibe-coding-here-is)). Listen for these in your preview.

## Step 6: ask for a change

Ask for one change at a time. It is easier to check. For example:

- "Put the opening hours in a list, one day per line."
- "Put our phone number and address at the top of the contact page."
- "The menu button only says 'Menu'. Make it say 'Show the list of cakes'."

The AI makes the change straight away, without a read-back, so say exactly what you want. When it is done, the result page tells you what changed. The site works this out itself by comparing the two versions, so it does not depend on what the AI says it did. The checks run again on the new version. Listen to the changed part in the preview.

If a change made things worse, say what went wrong in plain words, the same way you would tell a person.

## Step 7: ask for a peer check

Machine checks and the AI cannot tell you what a screen reader user actually experiences. Kelly Ford, a screen reader user for decades who built an email app with AI, put it this way: "AI can implement accessibility patterns. Only a screen reader user can verify that they actually work." ([source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/kellylford/QuickMail/main/docs/planning/the-quickmail-story.md))

In [Peer checks](/checks), ask other blind members to test your website with their own screen reader and device. Say what you want them to try, for example "order a cake from start to finish". Their findings are recorded honestly, including what they could not test. When you can, check someone else's work too. That is how the community keeps working without staff.

## When you are ready to share it

- Read the summary of the latest version once more.
- Make sure every peer check finding you care about is fixed.
- Then share the link with the people it is for.

If you get stuck at any step, [ask the community](/help/ask?title=Help%20with%20my%20first%20website).
