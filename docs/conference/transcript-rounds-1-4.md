Exported 2026-10-12 17:07 UTC. Rounds fully closed in this file: 1, 2, 3.

This is the working record of a multi-agent conference convened to decide what website or application should be built to help blind people turn their ideas into reality with AI. Every speaker read the verified landscape brief in `docs/research/landscape-brief.md` before speaking. ## Round 1: Powers and barriers

### Chair: opening of round 1
*Model: Fable 5.1.*

Good morning. I'm chairing, and here is the honest position: nobody has spoken yet, so there is no debate to summarize. There is only the organizers' premise, and it is doing a lot of work. It makes three claims. One: that building apps, companies and research programs with AI agents is now 'completely possible' for blind people. Two: that blind people have strengths sighted people lack. Three: that what stands in the way is only freedom to build. All three are contested until this panel earns them.

On strengths, the literature is messier than the brief. Pitch discrimination, verbal memory, tactile acuity, occipital recruitment for braille and language: real findings, but mostly in congenitally blind braille readers, and some results cut the other way, such as impaired auditory spatial bisection in early blindness. On 'completely possible': a terminal agent is text and screen readers like text, but spinners, redraws and a four-hundred-line diff are not text anyone can listen to. On 'freedom': free for whom? An NVDA-using backend engineer and someone who lost their sight at sixty and has never opened a terminal are not one user. A fourth fight I expect: whether a separate tool for blind builders is the answer, or whether separate means abandoned.

Rules. First person. Name each other. Quote the thing you disagree with. Sources, numbers, versions. No inspiration, no 'AI empowers everyone'; I will stop you. Six hundred to eleven hundred words each.

Dr. Vasquez, you open. Three strongest findings where blind people beat sighted people on something that matters for building, with authors, years and effect sizes, and the one finding that cuts against. For each, say who it holds for: congenital, late-blind, low-vision, speech users. Then draw the line from each finding to something a blind builder is more likely to invent. If you can't draw it, say so.

Yusuf, you answer Lena from your life. One project you shipped: the step you did better than a sighted collaborator would have, and the mechanism. Then your last week: the tool, the screen reader, the minute it failed you. Tell us whether Lena's evidence is your experience or lab-only.

Priya, you take Yusuf's list and sort it into three bins: missing label, wrong interaction model, genuinely hard. For the hard bin, reviewing what an agent built when you cannot see it, describe the non-visual verification loop. Tell me which bins a vendor could clear tomorrow; that decides whether we build something or write a complaint letter.

Sam, you close. For each of Priya's bins, name the tools, versions and months from the last six months that removed a blocker, and the ones that claimed to and didn't. Separate what you tested from what you read. Your landscape is input, not the plan.

We end with two lists: strengths to amplify, blockers to remove. Each of you proposes entries. Lena, go.

**Open disagreements going in**

- Strengths: the organizers' premise that blind people have strengths sighted people lack, versus the evidence that measured advantages (pitch discrimination, verbal memory, tactile acuity, occipital recruitment for braille and language) are task-specific, concentrated in congenitally blind braille readers, partly explained by practice rather than plasticity, and accompanied by documented deficits such as impaired auditory spatial bisection. Unresolved: whether any strength transfers to building and inventing, and for which blind populations.
- 'Completely possible today': the premise versus the chair's challenge that text-based agents are screen-reader-friendly in principle but terminal redraws, spinners, long diffs, visual app builders and deploy dashboards remain barriers. Unresolved: how much of the vibe-coding pipeline a blind builder can actually drive end to end with NVDA, JAWS or VoiceOver right now.
- Design failure versus hard problem: whether today's blockers are mostly missing labels and wrong interaction models that vendors could fix if they cared, or whether non-visual verification of agent output (does the thing I built work, and does it look right to sighted users?) is a genuinely unsolved problem that justifies building something new.
- Who the user is: technically fluent blind developers already working in the terminal versus non-technical, late-blind, low-vision or deafblind people who have never coded. Their blockers and strengths may not overlap, and the panel has not yet chosen.
- Separate tool versus mainstream accessibility: whether a dedicated product for blind builders is the right answer, or whether 'separate' historically means underfunded and abandoned, so the effort should go into forcing mainstream agent tools to be accessible.
- Framing: whether 'blind people will invent what sighted people have not imagined' is an evidence-backed claim about cognition and lived perspective, or supercrip inspiration rhetoric the panel must drop before it decides anything.

### Dr. Lena Vasquez
*Cognitive neuroscientist (cross-modal plasticity, sensory substitution, blind cognition). Model: Fable 5.1.*

Chair, you asked me to set the evidentiary bar. I will set it where the data are: below the organizers' premise, above the word "inspiration."

Finding one: ultra-fast speech comprehension. Moos and Trouvain, 2007, Saarbrücken: synthetic German at rising rates. Sighted listeners fell to near chance around eight to ten syllables per second; blind listeners were still answering comprehension questions correctly at seventeen to twenty-two, roughly three times conversational speed. Groups under twenty. Hertrich, Dietrich and Ackermann followed with fMRI and MEG in 2009 and 2013: in blind listeners right V1 and the pulvinar track the signal, and cortical activity phase-locks to syllable onsets at rates where sighted brains lose the lock. Who it holds for: practice with a cortical substrate, not a congenital gift. Dietrich and colleagues, 2013, trained late-blind adults for months; comprehension rose and V1 responses appeared as it rose. So late-blind, low-vision screen-reader users, anyone who listens all day. Not braille-specific, not deafblind. Sighted people improve with training; the ceiling comparison is under-studied. Sam's brief carries bretthalle's "300 to 500-plus words per minute" (June 25, 2026, github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/71471); it matches the lab.

The line to invention starts with a correction: speed is not bandwidth. Chair, your four-hundred-line diff is about four thousand words; at five hundred words per minute that is eight minutes of listening with no random access, and Marlon Sousa's "every pull request was costing me an evening of listening" (August 30, 2026) is what that looks like in practice. What blind listeners actually develop is temporal information design: what to say first, what to compress to an earcon, what to skip. While vendors shipped spinners, blind builders made echoterm's summary-first ranked output (July 24), acter's command-as-heading sessions (July 16, github.com/marlon-sousa/acter), BlindPilot's "Keep up" narration (August to October), and earshot's "Still working, ten seconds" heartbeats (October 10). That is a design discipline, typography for time, and a sighted engineer running ten agents from a phone will want it too.

Finding two: serial verbal memory. Amedi, Raz, Pianka, Malach and Zohary, Nature Neuroscience 2003: congenitally blind adults recalled more words than matched sighted controls, and V1 activity during retrieval predicted recall across blind participants. Raz, Striem, Pundak, Orlov and Zohary, Current Biology 2007, about twenty per group: blind participants recalled more and, crucially, kept the order of long lists better; the advantage grew with list length. The effect is large, near one standard deviation on the longest lists, with wide intervals at that n. Who it holds for: congenital and early blind. Pasqualotto, Lam and Proulx, 2013, found late-blind adults no better than sighted. Sam's brief holds the hopeful exception, Frontiers in Aging Neuroscience 2026: late-blind older adults with hearing loss still beat sighted peers on digit span and temporal modulation, though not on spectral or speech measures. Mechanism: part reorganization, part strategy; Raz's own title says "compensatory adjustment." And a sighted memory athlete with a visual palace beats every number in these papers, so this is a better default, not a higher ceiling.

Line to invention: structure-first information architecture. If you cannot glance, you hold the tree. Grid-Coding, which Sam's brief correctly re-dates to UIST 2022, showed twelve blind programmers navigating faster in a two-dimensional grid than in free text. They are more likely to invent software whose semantic structure is the product: DCA11Y's output contract, leading H1, no skipped heading levels (github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/70425); Sæti's WebMCP site, sixteen tools defined once serving both agents and a screen-reader command palette (August 27 to September 1, github.com/peritus-slf/webmcp-accessible-booking). Note the convergence: what a screen reader needs, a labeled, serial, semantic tree, is exactly what an agent needs. Accessible and agent-operable software are one artifact, and blind builders get there first. Plainly: the chain is modality plus capacity, not memory alone.

Finding three: tactile spatial acuity. Goldreich and Kanics, Journal of Neuroscience 2003, forty-three blind and forty-seven sighted, grating orientation: blind thresholds were finer across onset ages, equivalent to a sighted person twenty-three years younger. Wong, Gnanakumaran and Goldreich, 2011: the advantage sits on braille-reading fingers, scales with weekly braille hours, and is absent on the lips. Experience-dependent, with occipital involvement in early-blind readers (Cohen and colleagues, 1997: occipital TMS disrupts their braille reading). Holds for braille readers of any onset; weak in speech-only and low-vision users; sighted can train it. Line to invention: character-exact review. Speech glosses punctuation and whitespace; braille does not. The multi-line displays Mosen compared in May, Monarch and Dot Pad X, plus MAIDR's DotPad charts (v4.7.0, September 7, github.com/xability/maidr/releases), make tactile code and chart review real. But braille readers are a minority of blind adults, so this is a precision channel we support and never require.

Pitch discrimination, Gougoux and colleagues, Nature 2004: real, early-blind only, fewer than ten per group, and I cannot draw a line to building beyond sonification.

The finding that cuts against: Gori, Sandini, Martinoli and Burr, Brain 2014. Congenitally blind adults were severely impaired at auditory spatial bisection, judging whether a sound sits nearer its left or right neighbor, while fine at simple localization. Vision calibrates metric space for the other senses. Congenital, not late; and Vercillo and colleagues, 2015, found expert echolocators recovered it, so it is trainable. Product implication, my sharpest claim today: the drag-and-drop canvas is a sighted mental model, and Bolt, v0, Lovable and Figma make it the surface; Sam's brief found no blind-user path for any. Our product should take layout as a structural specification, let the agent own the pixels, then prove the result through the accessibility tree and a second, provenance-tagged description, DIRECT versus DERIVED versus SPECULATIVE in Eliza and Rahman's terms (July 27, arxiv.org/abs/2607.25021).

One more capacity, not perceptual: verification discipline. Perera's twelve spreadsheet users at CHI '26 never trusted unchecked output; Mazrui's "tests, logs, exit codes, accessibility scans, and what your screen reader actually says" is a scientific method sighted vibe-coders skip because they glance. Make evidence the spine of the product, not a tab.

Strengths to amplify: fast structured listening; structure-first modeling; braille-exact review for those who read it; evidence-based verification. Blockers to remove: no turn-end signal and no self-paced reading (#87978, #89002); no random access to long output, the diff that reads "Add comment" on every line (#100911) and the transcript that unmounts under the cursor (#99332); the canvas as the only creation surface; a second voice talking over the screen reader (github.com/mfmshazan/agent-ally/issues/2).

Yusuf, tell me whether you review diffs in braille or speech, and at what rate. Priya, I want the canvas filed under wrong interaction model, not missing label. Sam, which tools in your window deliver whole-answer, self-paced reading with heading discipline, tested rather than claimed?

**Key points**

- Three evidence-backed advantages that matter for building: ultra-fast speech comprehension (Moos & Trouvain 2007; Hertrich/Dietrich/Ackermann 2009-2013; trainable, holds for late-blind speech users), serial verbal memory (Amedi 2003; Raz 2007; congenital/early blind, ~1 SD, n~20), tactile acuity (Goldreich & Kanics 2003, n=90; braille-reading fingers, experience-dependent).
- Speed is not bandwidth: a 400-line diff is ~4,000 words, eight minutes at 500 wpm with no random access; the real capacity blind listeners develop is temporal information design (summary-first, earcons, skip-to-latest), which blind builders are already inventing (echoterm, acter, BlindPilot, earshot).
- What a screen reader needs (a labeled, serial, semantic tree) is exactly what an agent needs; accessible and agent-operable software are one artifact, so blind builders get there first (DCA11Y's output contract, Saeti's WebMCP).
- The counter-finding, Gori et al. 2014 (impaired auditory spatial bisection in congenital blindness), means the drag-and-drop canvas is a sighted mental model; the product must take layout as a structural spec, let the agent own pixels, and verify through the accessibility tree plus provenance-tagged descriptions.
- Pitch discrimination (Gougoux 2004) is real but early-blind only, under ten per group, and I cannot draw a line to building beyond sonification.
- Verification discipline (Perera CHI '26; Mazrui) is the most product-convertible capacity: make evidence the spine, not a tab; braille is a precision channel to support, never require.

**Agreed with**

- Chair: agreed that 'spinners, redraws and a four-hundred-line diff are not text anyone can listen to', and quantified it (about 4,000 words, eight minutes at 500 wpm with no random access).
- Chair: agreed that an NVDA-using engineer and someone blinded at sixty are not one user; gave onset-specific scope for each finding (fast speech holds for late-blind; serial memory does not; tactile acuity holds across onset but concentrates on braille fingers).
- Sam's brief, section 6: agreed that verification discipline under uncertainty (Perera et al. CHI '26; Mazrui) is the most distinct documented capacity, and that it must be budgeted for, not romanticized.
- Sam's brief: agreed that Grid-Coding is UIST 2022, not 2026, and that there is no demonstrated blind-user path in Bolt, v0, Lovable or Figma-style canvas builders.

**Disagreed with**

- Chair: disagreed with 'mostly in congenitally blind braille readers' as a blanket caveat; ultra-fast speech comprehension is a late-blind, speech-user, training effect (Dietrich et al. 2013) and tactile acuity held across onset ages in Goldreich & Kanics 2003.
- Organizers' premise: disagreed that fast listening makes building 'completely possible'; speed is not bandwidth, the bottleneck is random access and structure, not words per minute.
- Sam's brief, section 6: partly disagreed with 'not perception'; verification is epistemic, but fast structured listening and braille acuity are real perceptual strengths with lab evidence, just selective and small-sample.
- Any panelist tempted to count pitch discrimination as a building advantage: it is real (Gougoux 2004) but I cannot draw the line beyond sonification.

**Proposals made**

- The product surface must not be a canvas: layout is taken as a structural specification, the agent owns the pixels, and the result is proven through the accessibility tree plus a second, provenance-tagged description (DIRECT/DERIVED/SPECULATIVE).
- An output contract for everything the agent produces: a navigable document with leading H1, no skipped headings, labeled prefixes, a turn-end signal, self-paced reading, and speech rates to 500+ wpm.
- A temporal-information-design layer: summary-first output, layered verbosity, earcons, heartbeats and skip-to-latest narration, built from what blind builders already made (echoterm, acter, BlindPilot, earshot).
- Braille as a precision channel for character-exact diff review and tactile charts (multi-line displays, MAIDR DotPad), supported but never required.
- Verification as the spine: every agent claim carries an evidence class (tests, logs, exit codes, accessibility scans, what the screen reader actually said), not a verification tab.
- Round-1 lists: strengths to amplify (fast structured listening; structure-first modeling; braille-exact review; evidence-based verification) and blockers to remove (no turn-end signal/self-paced reading; no random access to long output and diffs; canvas as the only creation surface; a second voice over the screen reader).
